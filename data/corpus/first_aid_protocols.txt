# First Aid Protocols — Condensed Reference (Pulse Seed Corpus)

## Triage Levels — Decision Framework

### Emergency (Call Emergency Services Immediately)
- Chest pain, especially radiating to left arm, jaw, or back
- Difficulty breathing at rest or sudden onset
- Unresponsiveness or loss of consciousness
- Severe allergic reaction with throat swelling (anaphylaxis)
- Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes
- Signs of stroke: sudden weakness on one side, speech difficulty, facial droop (FAST)
- Severe bleeding that won't stop with direct pressure
- Suspected spinal injury — do not move the patient
- Source: Red Cross First Aid Guidelines, 2023

### Urgent (Seek Medical Care Within Hours)
- High fever (>39.5°C / 103°F) lasting more than 48 hours
- Severe headache with visual changes, stiff neck, or confusion
- Blood in urine, stool, or vomit
- Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, sunken eyes, rapid heartbeat, no urination 8+ hours
- Suspected fracture: deformity, inability to bear weight, severe swelling
- Deep or gaping wound that may need stitches
- Persistent vomiting or diarrhea (>24 hours) preventing fluid intake
- New confusion or behavioral change in elderly
- Source: Red Cross First Aid Guidelines, 2023

### Routine (Self-Care / Schedule Clinic Visit)
- Common cold symptoms: runny nose, mild cough, sneezing
- Minor cuts and scrapes: clean, apply antiseptic, bandage
- Mild headache without other symptoms
- Mild stomach upset, indigestion
- Muscle aches after physical activity
- Seasonal allergy symptoms: itchy eyes, sneezing
- Source: Red Cross First Aid Guidelines, 2023

## Symptom Assessment — Red Flags

### Headache Red Flags (seek urgent/emergency care if present)
- "Worst headache of my life" or thunderclap onset
- Headache with fever and stiff neck (meningitis concern)
- Headache with visual changes or blurred vision
- Headache after head trauma
- Headache with new neurological symptoms (weakness, numbness, speech change)
- Progressive worsening over days despite treatment
- Source: WHO Emergency Triage Guidelines

### Chest Pain Red Flags
- Pain radiating to arm, jaw, or back
- Associated with sweating, nausea, or shortness of breath
- Crushing or pressure-like quality
- New onset in patient with cardiac risk factors
- Pain not relieved by rest or position change
- Source: WHO Emergency Triage Guidelines

### Breathing Difficulty Red Flags
- Cannot speak in full sentences
- Blue lips or fingertips (cyanosis)
- Using accessory muscles to breathe (neck, ribs retracting)
- Respiratory rate >30 breaths/minute in adults
- Sudden onset after exposure to allergen (possible anaphylaxis)
- Source: WHO Emergency Triage Guidelines

## Drug Safety — General Principles

### Before Taking Any Medication
- Check for known allergies
- Review current medication list for interactions
- Follow prescribed dosing — more is NOT better
- Take medications with water unless directed otherwise
- Store medications properly (cool, dry, away from children)
- Source: WHO Patient Safety Guidelines

### Common Medication Cautions
- Never combine multiple pain relievers without medical advice
- Antibiotics: complete the full course even if feeling better
- Blood thinners: watch for unusual bruising or bleeding
- Diabetes medications: monitor blood sugar regularly; carry glucose tablets
- Blood pressure medications: do not stop abruptly
- Source: WHO Patient Safety Guidelines

### When to Seek Help About Medications
- Unexpected side effects (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing)
- Symptoms worsen despite taking medication as prescribed
- Accidental overdose or missed doses of critical medications
- New symptoms after starting a new medication
- Source: WHO Patient Safety Guidelines

# WHO Essential Medicines — Condensed Reference (Pulse Seed Corpus)

## Analgesics & Anti-inflammatories

### Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
- Standard dose: 500mg-1000mg every 4-6 hours, max 4g/day
- Hepatotoxicity risk above 4g/day or with chronic alcohol use
- Safe in pregnancy; first-line for mild-moderate pain
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

### Ibuprofen
- Standard dose: 200-400mg every 4-6 hours, max 1200mg/day (OTC)
- Contraindicated with active GI ulcer, severe renal impairment, third trimester pregnancy
- Increases bleeding risk when combined with anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin)
- NSAIDs may reduce effectiveness of antihypertensive medications (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, CCBs)
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

### Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid)
- Low-dose (75-100mg) for cardiovascular prevention
- Analgesic dose: 300-600mg every 4-6 hours
- Never give to children under 16 (Reye's syndrome risk)
- Increased bleeding risk with warfarin and other anticoagulants
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

## Cardiovascular Medications

### Amlodipine
- Calcium channel blocker for hypertension and angina
- Standard dose: 5-10mg once daily
- Common side effects: ankle edema, flushing, headache
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen) may reduce antihypertensive effect
- Blurred vision may indicate hypertensive crisis if on amlodipine
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

### Lisinopril
- ACE inhibitor for hypertension and heart failure
- Standard dose: 10-40mg once daily
- Contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic)
- Risk of hyperkalemia — avoid potassium supplements unless monitored
- Dry cough is a common class side effect (10-15% of patients)
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

### Atenolol
- Beta-blocker for hypertension and angina
- Standard dose: 25-100mg once daily
- Masks symptoms of hypoglycemia in diabetic patients
- Do not stop abruptly — risk of rebound hypertension and tachycardia
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

## Anticoagulants

### Warfarin
- Narrow therapeutic index; INR monitoring required (target 2.0-3.0)
- Numerous drug interactions: NSAIDs, antibiotics, antifungals increase bleeding risk
- Vitamin K-rich foods (leafy greens) reduce effectiveness
- Signs of over-anticoagulation: easy bruising, blood in urine/stool, prolonged bleeding
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

## Diabetes Medications

### Metformin
- First-line oral therapy for type 2 diabetes
- Standard dose: 500mg-2000mg daily in divided doses
- Take with food to reduce GI side effects
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30)
- Hold before iodinated contrast procedures (lactic acidosis risk)
- Alcohol increases lactic acidosis risk
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

### Insulin
- Types: rapid-acting (lispro, aspart), short-acting (regular), intermediate (NPH), long-acting (glargine)
- Hypoglycemia symptoms: tremor, sweating, confusion, tachycardia
- Beta-blockers may mask hypoglycemia symptoms
- Store unopened vials in refrigerator; opened vials stable at room temperature 28 days
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

## Mental Health

### Fluoxetine (Prozac)
- SSRI antidepressant; first-line for depression and anxiety
- Takes 2-4 weeks for full therapeutic effect
- NEVER combine with MAOIs (serotonin syndrome — potentially fatal)
- Serotonin syndrome risk with tramadol, other serotonergic drugs
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition

### Lithium
- Mood stabilizer for bipolar disorder
- Very narrow therapeutic window; serum monitoring essential
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen) reduce lithium clearance — toxic levels possible
- Maintain consistent fluid and salt intake
- Source: WHO Essential Medicines List, 2023 Edition
